Kangra is a historic city and district located in the North Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. Nestled in the scenic backdrop of the Dhauladhar mountain range, it is one of the most culturally rich and geographically diverse regions in the Himalayas.

## Key Highlights of Kangra

## 1. Rich History & Lineage

Trigarta Kingdom: Historically known as Nagarkot or Trigarta, the region finds mention in ancient Indian epics like the Mahabharata.

Katoch Dynasty: Kangra was ruled by the Katoch dynasty, considered one of the world's oldest surviving royal lineages.

Kangra Fort: Perched above the confluence of the Banganga and Majhi rivers, it is one of the oldest and largest forts in India. Over centuries, it withstood sieges from invaders like Mahmud of Ghazni, Firuz Shah Tughlaq, and Mughal emperors before eventually being heavily damaged by an earthquake in 1905.

## 2. Spiritual & Cultural Hub

Brajeshwari Devi Temple: Located in Kangra town, this famous Shakti Peeth is dedicated to Goddess Vajreshwari.

Masroor Rock-Cut Temples: A cluster of 8th-century monolithic temples carved out of a single rock, often called the "Ellora of Himachal".

Tibetan Culture (Dharamshala & McLeod Ganj): Dharamshala, the administrative headquarters of Kangra district, is home to the Dalai Lama and the Central Tibetan Administration, making the valley a global center for Tibetan Buddhism and arts.

## 3. Arts & Local Specialties

Kangra Miniature Paintings: The region gave birth to the famous Kangra school of art during the 18th century, renowned for intricate details, lyrical lines, and depictions of Lord Krishna and nature.

Kangra Tea: Known for its distinct floral aroma and flavor, Kangra tea holds a protected Geographical Indication (GI) tag.

## 4. Adventure & Tourism

Bir Billing: Located nearby in the district, it is internationally recognized as one of the top paragliding spots in the world.

Trekking: Popular routes like the Triund Trek offer views of the snow-capped Dhauladhar peaks.

In modern times, Kangra exists as both a district and a sub-divisional town within Himachal Pradesh, governed under India's administrative setup:

## Administrative Hierarchy & Structure

District Headquarters: Dharamshala serves as the administrative seat for the entire Kangra district.

District Leadership: Headed by a Deputy Commissioner (DC)—an IAS officer who handles administration, land revenue, and overall development—and a Superintendent of Police (SP) for law and order.

Divisional Level: Kangra district falls under the larger Kangra Division, which also includes Chamba and Una districts.

## Local Sub-Divisions & Governance

Sub-Divisions & Tehsils: The district is divided into 14 Sub-Divisions (e.g., Kangra, Dharamshala, Palampur, Nurpur) managed by Sub-Divisional Magistrates (SDMs). It comprises 19 Tehsils for revenue collection and local administration.

Civic Bodies: Kangra town itself is governed by a Municipal Council divided into local wards, responsible for civic amenities and local management.

Political Representation: Kangra is the most populous district in Himachal Pradesh, sending 15 MLAs to the State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) and holding a key seat in the Indian Parliament (Lok Sabha).

In the Kangra Division of Himachal Pradesh (which administratively encompasses Kangra, Chamba, and Una districts), temples are broadly divided into Government-Managed (State Temple Trusts) and Non-Government / Autonomous / ASI-Managed shrines.

## 1. Government-Managed Temples (State Temple Trusts)

These major pilgrimage sites are governed under the Himachal Pradesh Hindu Religious Institutions and Charitable Endowments Act. The local administration—headed by the Deputy Commissioner (DC) or Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM)—acts as the official commissioner or trustee of these temple boards.

## Major State Trusts in Kangra District

Shri Jawalamukhi Mata Temple (Jawalamukhi): One of the 51 Shakti Peethas, famous for the eternal natural gas flames burning without fuel. Managed by the Jawalamukhi Temple Trust board.

Shri Bajreshwari Devi Temple (Kangra Town): An ancient Shakti Peetha located in the heart of Kangra town, restored after the 1905 earthquake. Managed by the Kangra Temple Trust board.

Shri Chamunda Nandikeshwar Dham (Dadh / Palampur): Located on the banks of the Baner River with views of the Dhauladhar range. Managed by the state-run Chamunda Temple Board.

Aadi Himani Chamunda Temple: High-altitude mountain shrine above Dharamshala, managed in tandem with the primary Chamunda Devi trust.

## Major State Trusts in Other Division Districts (Una & Chamba)

Mata Shri Chintpurni Devi Temple (Una District): Falls under Kangra Administrative Division; one of the highest revenue-generating government-managed temple trusts in North India.

Chaurasi Temple Complex & Lakshminarayan Temple (Bharmaur & Chamba): Managed by district administration trust committees in Chamba.

## 2. Non-Government, Autonomous & ASI-Managed Temples

These temples are not run by state government trust boards. Instead, they are maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), local private/hereditary trusts, or community committees.

## Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) Protected Shrines

Baijnath Shiv Temple (Baijnath): A 13th-century Nagara-style stone temple dedicated to Lord Shiva (Vaidyanath). Maintained directly by the ASI as a national monument, with traditional priest families conducting daily worship.

Masroor Rock-Cut Temples: An 8th-century monolithic rock shrine complex ("Himalayan Pyramid") maintained and protected by the ASI.

Ambika Devi & Jain Temples (Kangra Fort): Located inside Kangra Fort, preserved by the ASI in coordination with the royal Katoch dynasty heritage trust.

## Private Trusts, Mahant Lineages & Local Committees

Maa Bagalamukhi Temple (Bankhandi): Managed by a local private trust and hereditary priest lineage (Mahant family). Highly popular for special havans and rituals.

Bhagsunag Temple (McLeod Ganj): Dedicated to Lord Shiva and the serpent deity; managed by local village committees and community trusts.

Kathgarh Shiv Temple (Indora): Famous for a unique split-Shivlinga that naturally changes distance based on seasons; managed by a local managing committee.

Radha Krishna Temple (Baba Baroh): Modern white-marble temple complex built and managed by a private community trust.

Bathu Ki Ladi (Pong Dam Reservoir): Submerged stone temples that emerge during dry months; managed informally by local committees and pilgrimage groups.
