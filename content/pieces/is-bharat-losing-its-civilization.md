Reflection :

1. For more than a century, these lines have inspired reflection on the endurance of Indian civilization. But in the 21st century, a new question confronts us—not whether Bharat can survive foreign invasions, but whether it can preserve its civilizational character while rapidly modernizing.

2. Iqbal's lines are not a history lesson—they are a civilizational question. What enables a civilization to endure through centuries of change?

## Is Bharat Losing Its Civilization?

## What Does Development Really Mean?

Over the last 35 years, Bharat has experienced remarkable economic growth. Our roads have improved, our cities have expanded, technology has transformed our lives, and millions have been lifted out of poverty. These are real achievements, and they deserve recognition.

But there is another question.

Is development only about increasing GDP?

If GDP rises, but families become weaker, loneliness increases, mental stress becomes common, wealth and power concentrate in a few hands, and a person's value is measured only by their ability to consume—can we truly call that complete development?

This is the question Bharat must ask today.

## How Do Civilizations Change?

No civilization disappears overnight.

It slowly changes its incentives.

When a society rewards strong families, families become stronger.

When it rewards only wealth and consumption, consumption gradually becomes its culture.

Civilizations move in the direction their incentives lead them.

## What Should We Learn from America—and What Should We Not?

America has given the world extraordinary contributions.

World-class universities, scientific research, the internet, modern computing, space technology, entrepreneurship, and innovation have transformed humanity.

But America has also faced serious social challenges.

Families have become smaller and, in many cases, less stable.

More people live alone than ever before.

Consumerism has become a dominant cultural force.

Large corporations have gained increasing influence across many sectors.

Concerns about mental health and social isolation continue to grow.

This is not a criticism of America.

It is simply a reminder that every model has both strengths and weaknesses.

## What Did We Inherit from England?

During colonial rule, Bharat adopted many British institutions—administration, law, bureaucracy, and education.

Many of these institutions proved useful.

But somewhere along the way, we also began to believe that modernity meant Westernization.

That was a subtle but important shift in our thinking.

## What Changed After Liberalization?

Economic liberalization in 1991 opened Bharat's economy to competition, investment, and global opportunities.

It accelerated growth and created new possibilities.

But it also reinforced a new social message.

A successful citizen is someone who earns more, buys more, and spends more.

Slowly, our definition of success began to change.

Earlier, society often asked:

"What kind of person are you?"

Today, it is more likely to ask:

"How much do you earn?"

## Is GDP Enough?

GDP is an important economic indicator.

But GDP does not tell us:

How strong our families are.

How much people trust one another.

Whether children are growing up in healthy environments.

Whether the elderly are living with dignity.

Whether people are mentally healthy.

Whether society is becoming more humane.

If a nation's GDP doubles but its social fabric is cut in half, can we truly call that progress?

## The Concentration of Wealth and Power

Today, not only Bharat but also America, China, and many other countries face an important question.

Is the modern economic system gradually concentrating wealth and power in the hands of a small number of corporations and institutions?

Large companies can certainly improve efficiency.

But if small businesses, local industries, and economic diversity continue to weaken, society may gradually lose both resilience and independence.

This is not only an economic question.

It is also a democratic one.

## Is Bharat Forgetting Its Greatest Strength?

Bharat's greatest strength has never been its economy alone.

Its real strength has always been:

Strong families,

Close-knit communities,

Cultural diversity,

Spiritual traditions,

Local knowledge,

And the ability to see life as more than a marketplace.

If these foundations weaken in the pursuit of economic growth, Bharat may become economically richer while becoming civilizationally poorer.

## What Is the Solution?

The solution is not to reject the West.

Nor is it to return to the past.

The solution is discernment.

Learn innovation from America.

Learn discipline from Japan.

Learn quality from Germany.

Learn strong public institutions from the Nordic countries.

Learn administrative efficiency from Singapore.

But do not take Bharat's soul away from Bharat.

The greatest mistake any nation can make is to lose its own identity while trying to imitate someone else's success.

## The Final Question

Today, we must ask ourselves:

Are we building an economy that serves human beings?

Or

Are we building a society where human beings exist primarily to serve the economy?

The answer to this question will determine whether future generations inherit merely a prosperous Bharat...

Or

A prosperous and living Indian civilization.
