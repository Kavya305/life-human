## The Three Viṣṇus: What Does the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Actually Say?

The idea of the “Three Viṣṇus” can appear complicated at first.

Are they three different Gods?

Are they three separate forms of Viṣṇu?

Or are they three manifestations of the same Supreme Reality functioning at different levels of creation?

The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam gives us a remarkably systematic description of this subject.

In its discussion of the Puruṣa, the Bhāgavatam describes the Lord's manifestation in relation to material creation through three forms:

Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

The sequence is essentially:

The many universes → the individual universe → the indwelling Lord

Let us look at what the Bhāgavatam says.

## 1. The Puruṣa appears at the beginning of creation

The description begins in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.3.1.

Sūta (Ugraśravā), the son of Romaharṣaṇa, was speaking to the sages gathered at Naimiṣāraṇya.

Sūta says that, at the beginning of creation, the Lord accepted a Pauruṣa form, the Puruṣa manifestation, in order to create the material universes.

The important word here is Puruṣa.

The Bhāgavatam is not beginning with an impersonal force. It presents the beginning of material creation through a divine Person who manifests the principles and ingredients required for cosmic creation.

The purport to this verse then explicitly identifies this first Puruṣa as Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, also called Mahāviṣṇu, and states that there are three Puruṣa features associated with material creation.

The other two are named as:

Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

This is therefore one of the clearest places in the Bhāgavatam tradition for understanding the threefold Puruṣa manifestation.

## 2. Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — Mahāviṣṇu

![](/uploads/karanodakasayi-vishnu-0c1ed0ca.webp)

Sri Karanodakasayi Visnu in Karana Ocean.

The first is Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, commonly called Mahāviṣṇu.

His level is the greatest scale of the three.

He is associated with the Causal Ocean, before the individual universes appear.

The Bhāgavatam describes Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī Viṣṇu as the first incarnation of the Supreme Lord in material creation and associates Him with the fundamental elements of cosmic manifestation—time, space, cause and effect, mind, material elements, the modes of nature, senses and the universal manifestation.

The picture that emerges is therefore not of one universe alone.

It is the cosmic level from which innumerable universes become manifest.

The traditional description associated with this form is extraordinary: countless universes arise from Mahāviṣṇu, while each individual universe subsequently receives another manifestation of the Puruṣa.

This gives us the first transition:

Mahāviṣṇu → countless universes

## 3. Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — the Lord within each universe

![](/uploads/garbhodakasayi-vishnu-68cb7bdb.webp)

Now the scale becomes smaller.

From the innumerable universes, we move into one individual universe.

The Bhāgavatam's description in 3.8 gives us a remarkable picture.

A lotus appears from Viṣṇu, and that lotus becomes the setting for the birth of Brahmā, the being who undertakes the work of universal creation.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 3.8.14 describes the lotus as being generated from the Personality of Viṣṇu.

Then 3.8.15 gives an especially important statement:

Viṣṇu personally enters the universal lotus as the Supersoul, and Brahmā is generated there.

The Bhāgavatam's commentary identifies this manifestation with Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, who enters each universe.

So the relationship can be understood as:

Mahāviṣṇu → many universes

and then:

Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu → enters each individual universe

Within that universe, the lotus associated with Viṣṇu becomes the birthplace of Brahmā.

This is an important distinction.

Brahmā is the creator within the universe, but the Bhāgavatam places Viṣṇu before and beneath that creative process.

Brahmā performs the work of universal creation; the source and foundation of that universe is Viṣṇu.

## 4. Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — the indwelling Lord

![](/uploads/ksirodakasayi-vishnu-9a281dfc.webp)

Now the Bhāgavatam takes us even further inward.

From the cosmic scale of countless universes, we have moved to one universe.

From one universe, we now move to the individual living being.

This is the domain associated with Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, identified with Paramātmā, the Supersoul.

The Bhāgavatam's description in 2.2.8 speaks of the Personality of Godhead residing within the region of the heart of the living being. The form described is four-armed and bears the familiar symbols of the Lord.

Thus, the third manifestation brings the cosmic principle into an intensely personal dimension.

The Divine is not only described as existing beyond the universe.

The Divine is also described as present within it.

And not merely within the universe in some abstract sense—the Bhāgavatam describes Paramātmā as residing within the heart.

This gives us the third movement:

Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu → Paramātmā within the living being

## 5. The complete progression

When these descriptions are placed together, an extraordinary progression emerges:

## 1. Mahāviṣṇu

The cosmic level

Associated with the manifestation of innumerable universes.

↓

## 2. Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

The universal level

Enters each individual universe and is associated with the lotus from which Brahmā is generated.

↓

## 3. Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

The indwelling level

Present as Paramātmā within the heart of the living being.

The Bhāgavatam's own explanatory tradition summarizes this threefold Puruṣa manifestation very clearly: the first is Kāraṇodakaśāyī, the second Garbhodakaśāyī, and the third Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu as Paramātmā.

## 6. Are these three different Gods?

This is where the terminology can sometimes create confusion.

The expression “Three Viṣṇus” should not be understood as three independent Gods competing with one another.

They are described as Puruṣa manifestations of the same Supreme Divine Reality, operating at different levels of material existence.

The first relates to the many universes.

The second relates to each universe.

The third relates to the indwelling presence within beings.

So the movement is:

Infinite cosmic scale

→ one universe

→ one living being

This is why the subject becomes philosophically interesting.

The Bhāgavatam is presenting a Divine Reality that can be contemplated both as the source of cosmic manifestation and as the indwelling presence within the individual.

## 7. The remarkable idea of “entering”

One word that becomes particularly important in these descriptions is entering.

The Lord does not merely create a universe and then remain distant from it.

The Bhāgavatam repeatedly describes the Lord as entering.

In 3.8.15, Viṣṇu enters the universal lotus as the Supersoul.

And the three-Puruṣa explanation describes the second manifestation as the Lord entering each universe, followed by the expansion of Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu as Paramātmā.

This produces a fascinating philosophical movement:

The transcendent becomes immanent.

The Divine is not merely the cause before creation.

The Divine is also described as present within creation.

And ultimately, within the heart.

## 8. From the universe to the heart

Perhaps the most beautiful way to understand the three Puruṣa manifestations is to see them as three scales of divine presence.

## The first question:

Where do the universes come from?

The Bhāgavatam points toward Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu.

## The second:

What is present within each universe?

The Bhāgavatam describes Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu entering each universe.

## The third:

Where is the Divine in relation to the living being?

The Bhāgavatam describes Paramātmā within the heart.

So the journey is:

Cosmos → Universe → Heart

Or:

Mahāviṣṇu → Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu → Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu

## 9. What the Bhāgavatam actually establishes

It is useful to make one distinction here.

Some popular explanations of the Three Viṣṇus contain details drawn from commentaries, other Purāṇic texts, Brahma-saṁhitā, Vaiṣṇava theology and later traditions.

Those explanations can be valuable, but we should not automatically present every detail as though it is a direct statement of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.

What the Bhāgavatam itself gives us very clearly is the framework:

The Puruṣa appears in relation to material creation.

Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu is the first Puruṣa.

Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu enters each universe.

The lotus associated with Viṣṇu becomes the setting for Brahmā's appearance.

Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu is associated with Paramātmā, the Lord within.

These ideas are found across Bhāgavatam 1.3, 2.2, 2.6 and 3.8, giving us a coherent picture rather than a single isolated verse.

## 10. One Reality — three cosmic levels

The Three Viṣṇus therefore offer more than a description of Hindu cosmology.

They present a particular way of thinking about the relationship between the Divine, the universe and the individual.

At the largest scale:

Viṣṇu is associated with the manifestation of countless universes.

At the level of the individual universe:

Viṣṇu enters and becomes its inner foundation.

At the level of the individual being:

Paramātmā is described as present within the heart.

The same Divine Reality is therefore contemplated from three different perspectives.

Perhaps that is the simplest way to remember the teaching:

> Mahāviṣṇu — the universes. Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — the universe. Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu — the indwelling Lord.

From the unimaginable scale of countless universes to the quiet space within the human heart, the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam presents a vision in which the Divine is never truly outside creation.

## Primary Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam References

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.3.1–2 — the Puruṣa manifestation and the progression of the three Puruṣas.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.2.8 — the Personality of Godhead residing in the region of the heart.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.6.42 — Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī Viṣṇu as the first incarnation associated with the cosmic principles and Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 3.8.14–15 — the lotus generated from Viṣṇu and Viṣṇu entering the universal lotus, with Brahmā being generated there.

## A note on terminology

The names Kāraṇodakaśāyī, Garbhodakaśāyī, and Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu belong to the established Vaiṣṇava theological vocabulary used to explain the Puruṣa manifestations. When presenting this subject historically or academically, it is therefore best to distinguish between the Bhāgavatam's verses themselves and the commentarial/theological explanations built around those verses.

That distinction makes the article stronger—not weaker—because we allow the reader to see exactly what the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam says and then understand how the larger Vaiṣṇava interpretation develops from it.
