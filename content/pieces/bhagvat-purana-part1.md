Bhagvat Purana - Srimad Bhagvatam Introduction

The Bhagavata Purana (also
known as the Srimad Bhagavatam) is one of the 18 major Mahapuranas in
Hindu literature. Composed in Sanskrit and attributed to Sage Ved Vyasa, it is
widely revered as the crown jewel of Puranic literature due to its poetic
depth, philosophical synthesis, and intense focus on selfless devotion (Bhakti).

Core Structure & Framework

The text is arranged as a
dialogue narrated by Sage Shukadeva to King Parikshit on the
banks of the Ganges. Parikshit, cursed to die in seven days from a snakebite,
asks what a person facing imminent death ought to focus on. Shukadeva’s answer
forms the entire Purana.

12
Cantos (Skandhas): Divided into 335 chapters containing roughly
18,000 verses.

10 Essential Themes (Lakshanas):
Unlike standard Puranas that cover 5 topics, the Bhagavata outlines 10:
creation (Sarga), secondary creation (Visarga), cosmic order
(Sthana), divine protection (Poshana), latent desires (Uti),
planetary cycles (Manvantara), science of God (Ishanukatha),
dissolution (Nirodha), liberation (Mukti), and the Ultimate
Refuge (Aashraya).

Key Highlights & Teachings

1. Krishna as Supreme (Swayam Bhagavan)

While other Puranas treat Krishna
as an avatar of Vishnu, the Bhagavata Purana asserts that Krishna is the
original source from whom all avatars descend ("Krsnastu bhagavan
svayam" – 1.3.28).

2. The 10th Canto (The Heart of the Text)

Accounting for nearly a quarter
of the entire work, Canto 10 narrates Krishna’s birth, childhood pastimes in
Vrindavan, defeat of demons, and interactions with the Gopis. It presents
divine love as the highest spiritual attainment.

3. Synthesis of Bhakti, Jnana, and Karma

The text integrates Advaita
(non-dualist) metaphysics with intense personal devotion. It emphasizes that
ultimate liberation is achieved not just through intellectual knowledge (Jnana),
but through total surrender and emotional connection to the Divine (Bhakti).

4. The 24 Gurus of Dattatreya (Canto 11)

Known as the Uddhava Gita,
this section features Lord Dattatreya explaining how he gained spiritual wisdom
by observing 24 subtle teachers in nature—including the earth, air, water,
spider, and honeybee.
