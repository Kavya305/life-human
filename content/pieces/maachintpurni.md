Among the great goddess shrines of northern India, Maa Chintpurni occupies a particularly important place in the religious landscape of Himachal Pradesh. Situated in the Shivalik hills of present-day Una district, the shrine attracts pilgrims from Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Jammu and Kashmir, Delhi and other parts of India.

The name Chintpurni itself expresses the central belief associated with the goddess: Chinta means worry or anxiety, while purni is associated with fulfillment or completion. Maa Chintpurni is therefore revered as the Mother who removes the worries of her devotees and fulfills their heartfelt prayers.

The temple is traditionally associated with Maa Chhinnamastika, a powerful form of the Goddess, and is also revered within the Shakta tradition as one of the sacred Shakti Peethas. The Government of Himachal Pradesh identifies the shrine with Goddess Bhagwati Chhinmastaka and records the long-standing tradition of the Chintpurni fair. (District Una)

## Where is Maa Chintpurni Temple?

Maa Chintpurni Temple is located at Chintpurni in Una district, Himachal Pradesh, on a hill range traditionally associated with the Solah Singhi hills. The shrine lies close to Bharwain and historically occupies an important position between the plains of Punjab and the Himalayan foothills.

The location is significant. Chintpurni is not deep inside the high Himalayas; instead, it sits in the transitional landscape where the northern Indian plains rise toward the Shivalik and Himalayan region.

This helped make the shrine accessible to pilgrims travelling from the Punjab plains as well as from the hill regions.

The official Una district website places Chintpurni approximately 3 km west of Bharwain and notes its road connections with Una, Hoshiarpur and Kangra. (District Una)

## The Ancient Religious Tradition

The history of Chintpurni has two different dimensions that should be kept separate:

Religious tradition and sacred mythology

Documented historical evidence concerning the temple and settlement

The mythology surrounding the shrine is much older than the surviving physical temple structure. As with many major Hindu pilgrimage centres, devotees understand the sacredness of the site through traditions preserved over generations.

According to local tradition, the Goddess appeared here in an astral or divine form in ancient times. The Government of Himachal Pradesh itself records this tradition in its description of the Chintpurni shrine. (District Una)

The goddess is traditionally identified as Chhinnamastika Devi, connecting Chintpurni with the powerful Shakta traditions surrounding the Goddess.

## Maa Chhinnamastika and the Meaning of the Goddess

One of the most distinctive aspects of Chintpurni is its association with Chhinnamastika.

The name Chhinnamastika is generally understood in relation to the imagery of the Goddess who has severed her own head. This is one of the most striking and profound forms of Shakti.

In Shakta and Tantric traditions, Chhinnamasta represents much more than a fierce deity.

Her imagery deals with profound ideas such as:

self-sacrifice

the destruction of ego

the relationship between life and death

the power of Shakti

liberation from worldly attachment

the relationship between the individual self and divine consciousness

The apparently frightening imagery therefore has a deeper philosophical meaning.

The Goddess gives her own life-force to nourish others. In this interpretation, Chhinnamasta represents the paradox of death producing life and sacrifice producing nourishment.

This is particularly appropriate for a Mother Goddess: even in her most terrifying form, she remains the source of life.

## The Legend of Chhinnamasta

A famous Shakta tradition tells of the Goddess after a great battle.

After the Goddess had defeated the forces of evil, her attendants became extremely hungry and demanded food.

Their hunger became so intense that the Goddess performed an extraordinary act of self-sacrifice. She severed her own head, and the streams of blood flowing from her body nourished her attendants while also sustaining herself.

The imagery is deliberately dramatic.

It teaches that Shakti is simultaneously the source, sustainer and receiver of life.

This tradition is particularly important when understanding why the deity at Chintpurni is called Chhinnamastika.

However, it is important to distinguish this religious tradition from archaeological history: the theological story explains the sacred identity of the Goddess, rather than providing a datable historical account of when the present temple was built.

## Chintpurni and the Shakti Peetha Tradition

Perhaps the most important reason for Chintpurni's fame is its association with the Shakti Peetha tradition.

According to the famous story of Sati, Daksha's daughter Sati married Lord Shiva. Daksha later conducted a great sacrifice but deliberately excluded Shiva.

Sati went to the sacrifice despite Shiva's warning.

There she was insulted, and unable to tolerate the humiliation of her husband, she gave up her body in the sacrificial fire.

Shiva was overcome with grief and anger. Carrying Sati's body, he began the cosmic dance of destruction.

To restore cosmic balance, Vishnu is traditionally said to have divided Sati's body with his discus. Different parts of her body fell at different locations across the Indian subcontinent.

These places became the Shakti Peethas, sacred centres of Shakti worship.

Chintpurni is traditionally counted among these sacred sites.

## An important historical qualification

There are different traditional lists of Shakti Peethas, and the body part associated with a particular shrine can vary between textual traditions. Therefore, when discussing Chintpurni in a historical article, it is better to say that the shrine is traditionally regarded as a Shakti Peetha, rather than presenting one particular version of the legend as an archaeological fact.

## Why is She Called "Chintpurni"?

The name itself explains much of the devotion surrounding the temple.

Chinta = worry, anxiety or concern.

Purni/Purti = fulfillment or completion.

Maa Chintpurni is therefore understood as the Goddess who removes the worries of her devotees.

For generations, people have visited the shrine with personal prayers concerning:

family

marriage

children

livelihood

health

financial difficulties

journeys

success

protection from difficulties

This has made the shrine deeply personal for devotees.

A pilgrim does not necessarily come only to witness an ancient monument. Many come because they believe they can place their worries before the Mother.

That emotional relationship between devotee and Goddess is one of the central reasons for the enduring popularity of Chintpurni.

## The Founder Tradition of the Temple

Local traditions connect the development of the shrine with Mai Das, who is traditionally regarded as an important early devotee associated with the establishment of the temple.

However, the exact chronology of the earliest shrine is difficult to establish with certainty from surviving historical evidence.

This is an important point when writing about Chintpurni's history.

The sacred tradition is ancient, but that does not necessarily mean that the current temple building itself is thousands of years old.

The physical shrine has almost certainly undergone changes, repairs and reconstruction over the centuries as pilgrimage to the site expanded.

Thus, it is better to understand Chintpurni as an ancient sacred centre whose physical temple has evolved over time.

## Chintpurni in the Landscape of Kangra and Punjab

The geographical position of Chintpurni is particularly interesting when looking at its history.

The region around present-day Una was historically connected with the wider cultural world of Kangra, the Shivaliks and the Punjab plains.

Pilgrims travelling north from Punjab could reach the Himalayan foothills and then continue toward other important goddess shrines.

Chintpurni therefore became part of a larger sacred landscape that includes famous temples such as:

Jwalamukhi

Brajeshwari Devi at Kangra

Chamunda Devi

Naina Devi

Chintpurni

These shrines collectively helped make the western Himalayan region one of the great centres of Devi worship in northern India.

## The Chintpurni Fair

The religious importance of the shrine can also be seen in its fairs.

The Government of Himachal Pradesh describes the traditional Chintpurni fair, locally called Mata Da Mela, as a long-standing religious gathering.

The major fairs take place three times a year:

## Chet — March/April

The fair coincides with the Navratras.

## Sawan — July/August

The fair takes place during the first ten days of the bright half of Sawan.

## Asanj — September/October

Another major Navratra fair is held.

The official district account says that the Navratra fairs traditionally continue for nine days, while the Sawan fair lasts ten days. (District Una)

These festivals transform the quiet hill settlement into a major pilgrimage centre.

## Navratri at Chintpurni

Navratri is perhaps the most important period of the religious calendar.

During Navratri, devotees worship the Goddess through:

fasting

prayers

bhajans

devotional singing

offerings

temple visits

circumambulation

charitable activities

The atmosphere is particularly intense because Chintpurni is not an isolated temple visited only by local residents. It becomes a meeting place for pilgrims from different regions of northern India.

For many families, visiting Maa Chintpurni is an inherited tradition.

Parents bring their children, who later bring their own children.

In this way, pilgrimage becomes a form of living cultural memory.

## The Goddess as a Mother

Theologically, Maa Chintpurni represents one of the most important ideas in Hindu Shaktism:

The ultimate divine power can be approached as Mother.

A devotee does not necessarily approach the Goddess as a distant cosmic force.

Instead, she is:

Mata — Mother

protector

giver

remover of fear

fulfiller of wishes

source of strength

This maternal relationship is visible in the language devotees use when speaking about Chintpurni.

The shrine is therefore not simply a monument.

It is a living centre of devotion.

## Why Chintpurni is Important to Punjab

Although the temple lies in Himachal Pradesh, its religious importance extends deeply into Punjab.

The shrine is relatively close to the Punjab plains, and generations of Punjabi families have travelled to Chintpurni.

This geographical connection helped establish a cultural corridor between:

Punjab → Hoshiarpur → Chintpurni → Kangra → Jwalamukhi

Pilgrimage along these routes helped spread stories, songs, traditions and family connections across the region.

The temple therefore belongs not only to the religious history of Una but to the broader cultural history of Punjab and the western Himalayas.

## Chintpurni in Modern Himachal Pradesh

Administratively, Chintpurni is now part of Una district.

Interestingly, Una itself became a separate district relatively recently. The Government of Himachal Pradesh states that on 1 September 1972, the former Kangra district was reorganised into three districts: Una, Hamirpur and Kangra. (District Una)

Thus, when discussing the medieval or early-modern history of Chintpurni, it would be anachronistic to describe it as being historically part of a modern "Una district."

The region belonged to older political and cultural landscapes associated with Kangra and the surrounding hill states and Punjab plains.

## Chintpurni Today

Today Maa Chintpurni is one of Himachal Pradesh's major pilgrimage destinations.

The shrine continues to attract large numbers of devotees throughout the year, with particularly large crowds during Navratri.

The importance of the temple has also resulted in continuing investment in infrastructure around the shrine. In recent years, government projects have focused on improving facilities and pilgrimage infrastructure. (The Times of India)

But despite modernization, the fundamental reason people visit remains remarkably old:

to stand before the Goddess and ask her to remove their worries.

## The Deeper Significance of Maa Chintpurni

The importance of Chintpurni can ultimately be understood on several levels.

## 1. Religious significance

It is a major centre of Shakti worship and is traditionally regarded as a Shakti Peetha.

## 2. Mythological significance

The shrine is associated with Sati, Shakti and the powerful form of Chhinnamastika.

## 3. Spiritual significance

Chhinnamastika's symbolism represents sacrifice, transformation, the destruction of ego and the power of divine consciousness.

## 4. Cultural significance

For generations, families from Himachal Pradesh, Punjab and neighbouring regions have maintained pilgrimage traditions connected with the shrine.

## 5. Historical significance

Chintpurni illustrates how sacred landscapes can survive political and administrative changes while retaining their cultural identity.

## 6. Emotional significance

Perhaps most importantly, the Goddess provides devotees with a place where they can bring their chinta — their worries.

That is why the name Chintpurni remains so meaningful.

## Conclusion

Maa Chintpurni is much more than an old temple in the hills of Himachal Pradesh.

She represents a long and continuing tradition of Shakti worship, connecting mythology, pilgrimage, family tradition and the cultural history of the western Himalayas.

The precise historical age of the earliest shrine is difficult to establish, and the legends surrounding the Goddess belong to the realm of sacred tradition rather than conventional archaeological history. Yet that distinction does not diminish the shrine's importance.

For centuries, generations of devotees have regarded this place as the home of a compassionate and powerful Mother.

The Goddess of Chintpurni is ultimately remembered for one simple promise expressed in her name:

the Mother who takes away the worries of those who come to her.

And perhaps that is the greatest reason why, generation after generation, the roads leading into the hills of Chintpurni continue to fill with pilgrims.
